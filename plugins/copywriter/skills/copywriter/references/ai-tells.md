# AI tells in customer copy

Patterns that make marketing copy read as generated. The drafter avoids them; the
`copy-reviewer` agent flags them. Each entry: the pattern, a real example, the fix.

One instance of a pattern is often fine. The tell is **repetition** and **vagueness**.

## 1. Contrast framing
"Not X, but Y" / "X, not Y" / "It's not just X — it's Y" / "More than just X."
- Example: "New to East Texas, not new to the trade."
- Fix: say the positive fact directly. "Washington crew, now working East Texas."

## 2. Tenure or quality with no number
"Years of experience", "decades of expertise", "behind every job", "seasoned crew",
"proven track record".
- Example: "A crew with years of excavation behind it."
- Fix: the real number ("Excavating since 2008"), or a `[YEARS]` placeholder. Never
  write around a missing fact.

## 3. One sentence template, many times
The same grammatical shape repeated across list items or sections.
- Example: "New ponds dug, and old ones cleaned out." / "New driveways cut, and old
  ones regraded." / "Debris hauled, and old structures taken down."
- Fix: keep one; rewrite the others as plain active sentences, fragments, or a
  different angle (the problem it solves, a real job).

## 4. Uniform structure
Every bullet the same length; every list exactly three items (triads like "fast,
reliable and affordable"); parallel lists on sibling pages with identical counts.
- Fix: let lists be lumpy. One long detail, one short fragment. Different lengths
  on different pages.

## 5. Filler vocabulary
straightforward, seamless, robust, tailored, comprehensive, top-notch, quality
(as an adjective with nothing behind it), hassle-free, peace of mind, elevate,
unlock, navigate, landscape, journey, dedicated to, committed to, your trusted
partner, look no further, we've got you covered, whether you're X or Y,
"the X the region is known for".
- Fix: delete the word and see if the sentence still works. If not, replace it with
  a fact.

## 6. Repeated call to action or claim
The same CTA phrase ("free estimate") three or more times on a page; the same claim
("now based in Branson") in two sections.
- Fix: a CTA phrase at most twice per page (usually the button and the form). Each
  claim once.

## 7. Polished closers
Aphorisms or punchlines ending a section: "Because your land deserves better."
"Built right, the first time." Rhetorical openers: "Looking for X?"
- Fix: at most one memorable line per page, and only if it carries a real fact
  ("we plan for rock up front, so the estimate holds when the bucket hits limestone").

## 8. Invented specifics
Testimonials, stats, percentages, awards, licenses, guarantees, response times or
service areas that are not in the fact sheet.
- Fix: cut it or placeholder it (`[TESTIMONIAL]`). This is a blocker, not a style note.

## 9. Punctuation cadence
Em dashes in most paragraphs; every bullet in "Label: explanation" form; colons
introducing every list.
- Fix: vary it. Most sentences should be plain subject-verb-object.
